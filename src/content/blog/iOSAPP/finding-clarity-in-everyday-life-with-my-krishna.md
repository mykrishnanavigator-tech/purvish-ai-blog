*A difficult conversation. A disappointing day. A moment to reflect.*

The conversation ended hours ago, but you’re still replaying it.

Why did they say that? Should you have responded differently? Should you send another message?

Other days, it’s something else. You worked hard, but the result disappointed you. Someone else’s success made you question your own progress. You spent the day meeting everyone’s needs and reached bedtime wondering whether you gave your attention to the right things.

These are the kinds of everyday moments I want My Krishna to help people reflect on.

I built My Krishna as an AI reflection app inspired by Krishna’s teachings. Much of what I’ve shared so far has been about building the technology. But the more personal question is: **where could it fit into an ordinary day?**

Here are a few places to start.

## When a conversation stays with you

A colleague dismisses your suggestion. A family member makes a comment that hurts. You keep imagining what you should have said.

Before deciding what to do, it can help to examine what bothered you. Was it the disagreement itself, the way it was expressed, or the feeling that someone didn’t value you?

A question you could bring to My Krishna is:

> I keep replaying a conversation where I felt dismissed. Help me reflect on my reaction through Krishna’s teachings.

Reflection might help you approach the next conversation with a clearer intention. You may still need to speak up or set a boundary. Taking a moment gives you room to consider how.

## When effort doesn’t bring the result you wanted

You prepared carefully. You put in the hours. You cared.

Then the promotion went to someone else. The project received little attention. Something you created didn’t reach the people you hoped it would.

Krishna’s teachings on action and attachment offer a useful starting point for exploring this disappointment: how can we remain committed to our effort while accepting that the outcome is never entirely ours to control?

That is difficult to practice when something matters deeply.

You could ask:

> I worked hard and feel disappointed with the outcome. How can I stay committed without letting this result define my worth?

From there, consider what the experience can teach you. What would you change next time? What deserves continued effort? What expectation are you struggling to release?

## When someone else always seems ahead

A few minutes of scrolling can make your own progress feel small.

Someone has a better job, a growing business, a happier-looking family, or the confidence you wish you had. Suddenly, a day you felt good about becomes evidence that you’re falling behind.

That feeling can be a reason to pause and ask what you actually want.

A reflection prompt might be:

> I’m comparing my progress with other people and feeling behind. Help me think about my own path and responsibilities.

Perhaps their achievement points toward something you genuinely value. Perhaps you’re measuring yourself against a life you wouldn’t choose.

Giving that distinction some attention can help you decide where to put your energy.

## When work and family both need you

There is another deadline, another message, another person waiting for your attention.

Even when you’re working, you feel guilty about family. When you’re with family, part of your mind stays at work.

Some days involve real conflicts between responsibilities. Reflection can help you name those conflicts and make a more deliberate choice.

You could ask:

> “I feel pulled between work and family today. Help me reflect on my responsibilities and choose one practical next step.”

That step might be finishing an urgent task, asking for help, or putting your phone away during dinner. A useful reflection should eventually connect with something you can do.

## When you’re about to send an angry message

You’ve written the reply. Your thumb is hovering over Send.

This is a small but meaningful opportunity to pause.

Ask yourself what you want the message to accomplish. Do you want to explain, resolve, defend a boundary—or make the other person feel what you’re feeling?

You could bring this question to My Krishna:

> “I’m angry and want to respond immediately. Help me examine my intention before I send this message.”

You may still decide to send a firm response. The pause gives you a chance to choose words you can stand behind tomorrow.

## When the day didn’t go well

At bedtime, it’s easy to turn a difficult day into a judgment about yourself.

You lost patience. You avoided something important. You didn’t accomplish what you planned.

Try giving yourself five minutes to reflect on one experience:

- What happened?
- What was within my control?
- What is one thing I can do differently tomorrow?

You could begin with:

> “Today didn’t go the way I hoped. Help me reflect honestly and identify one small action for tomorrow.”

Some evenings, one honest observation and a manageable next step may be enough.

## Start with what’s on your mind

You don’t need a perfectly worded question to begin reflecting. You can start with the sentence already in your head:

*“That comment hurt.”*

*“I’m disappointed.”*

*“I don’t know what deserves my attention today.”*

My hope for My Krishna is that it gives people an accessible way to explore those moments through Krishna’s teachings, then carry something thoughtful back into their lives.

**If something from your day is still on your mind, try bringing that question to My Krishna. Give yourself a few minutes to reflect—and decide what you want to do next.**
